<Aside type="caution" title="Capacity value is not cash savings">
  This estimator values time that may be redirected to other work. That value is not a staffing
  reduction, cash savings, or guaranteed financial return unless cash expense actually decreases.
</Aside>

This browser-only estimator shows the assumptions and formulas needed for an operator conversation.
Start with empty fields and add your own measured or quoted values. Use the **Load example** button
to see fabricated demonstration data — every example number is clearly labeled as assumed, not real.

## What the numbers mean

- **Hours potentially redirected**: Adopted task volume × positive minutes difference ÷ 60.
  Time that could become available for other work.
- **Gross labor capacity value**: Hours × loaded hourly labor cost. The notional value of
  redirected time if it were applied to productive work at the same rate.
- **Monthly cash cost**: Software/hosting plus other cash costs, adjusted for annual price
  increases. The actual cash leaving your account.
- **Monthly labor cost**: Maintenance hours and estimated downtime/incident time. Labor
  you or your team will spend operating the tooling.
- **Net monthly capacity value**: Gross capacity value minus total monthly operating cost.
  Positive means the estimated capacity value exceeds the cost to operate.
- **First-year capacity estimate**: Net monthly value × 12, minus all one-time costs (setup,
  training, migration, and contingency). A directional year-one picture.
- **Payback period**: How many months of net capacity value would recover total one-time
  costs. Not shown when the monthly net is zero or negative.

## Cash savings mode

Check "This tooling will reduce cash expense" only if you have a specific plan to reduce
staffing or cancel a contract. The estimator does not change its math — but the label
reminds you that capacity value and cash savings are different things.

## Formula boundaries

- All figures are estimates based on your inputs. Even "measured" values are samples,
  not guarantees.
- Adoption ramps, exceptions, downtime, migration, and training are estimated as overhead.
  Actual overhead will vary.
- Contingency is a percentage added to one-time costs. It covers unknowns, not known risks.
- This is not financial, accounting, tax, employment, or procurement advice. Verify every
  assumption with a bounded pilot before making decisions.
